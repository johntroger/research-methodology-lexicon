Assessing Measurement Invariance and Causal Mechanisms in Organizational Behavior

Journal of Empirical Research & Methodology, Vol. 48, 2026
Abstract This empirical study delineates a comprehensive framework for mitigating common method bias (CMB) in cross-sectional research designs. Using partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM), we substantiate the mediating role of psychological safety between transformative leadership and employee innovation. Robustness checks confirm that the statistical findings remain robust even when accounting for potential endogeneity.

1. Methodological Considerations

Establishing construct validity is of paramount importance before conducting structural model assessment. According to Creswell & Creswell (2023), researchers must ensure both convergent validity and discriminant validity. For convergent validity, average variance extracted (AVE) must exceed 0.50, and composite reliability (CR) should surpass 0.70.

For discriminant validity, recent methodological literature cautions against relying solely on the Fornell-Larcker criterion and recommends evaluating the heterotrait-monotrait ratio ([HTMT]). A stringent threshold of HTMT < 0.85 indicates that the constructs are empirically distinct. Furthermore, multicollinearity among exogenous indicators was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), which remained well below the conservative threshold of 3.3.

2. Qualitative Triangulation & Robustness

To achieve data saturation, we employed purposive sampling to recruit senior managers for in-depth interviews. We implemented member checking and methodological triangulation to enhance trustworthiness. A key caveat of this study is that external validity should be interpreted within the context of high-tech firms.
